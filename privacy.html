1. Terms of use (what you accept when you open Scanok)

The first time you open the app you accept these terms with one tap. Your phone stores the date and the version you accepted; if they change, you are asked again.

  1. Scanok is informative. It scans the barcode or reads the label of food, drinks, cosmetics, toothpastes, dog and cat food, supplements and medicines, and shows what public sources publish: the pack itself, Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts and Open Pet Food Facts, official medicine records (Spain’s AEMPS-CIMA and those of other EU countries), AESAN, EFSA, EU regulations and the guidelines and studies it cites in each case. From that data its own engine calculates a score with fixed, published criteria that are the same for every product. It puts it into plain words and compares it with what you have marked.
  2. No deals with anyone. Scanok has not signed and does not sign agreements with manufacturers, brands, supermarkets, laboratories, pharmacies, advertisers or any other company or body. Nobody pays to appear in Scanok or for how they appear, and Scanok takes no commission on any sale. “Better choice” suggestions come from the same engine, never from a payment. Its sources are public and cited with their licence; using them does not mean any of them endorses Scanok.
  3. Not a medical service or a medical device. The people behind Scanok never act as doctors, pharmacists, dentists, vets, nutritionists or religious authorities. Scanok does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease; it does not prescribe, recommend or advise against any medicine, supplement or treatment, and gives no doses. Its alerts say what a product contains according to its label or official record and whether it matches what you marked; they are not medical or veterinary advice.
  4. The pack, your doctor, your pharmacist and your vet come first. Source data may contain errors or be out of date. If in doubt, check the label and ask a professional. Never stop or change a treatment because of Scanok.
  5. Religious notes cite their sources (Sunnah and Qur’an; Shulchan Aruch and Leviticus) and do not replace the opinion of your imam or rabbi.
  6. No guarantee that everything is there. Scanok warns about what it finds; no warning does not guarantee a product does not contain something.
  7. What you contribute. If you add a missing product (with two photos of its pack), you allow Scanok to keep the name, brand and ingredient list read from those photos and to show them to whoever scans that product later. Do not send photos showing people or personal data: only the pack.
  8. Age. Scanok is meant for people aged 14 and over, the age at which you can consent to data processing yourself under Spanish law (Organic Law 3/2018, article 7). A child’s or baby’s data is entered by their parent or guardian.
  9. Use. Personal use only. You may not copy the app or its database to sell them, use them to mislead anyone, or force or overload the Scanok service. Third-party data keeps its licence (see the database and its licences).
  10. Intellectual property. Scanok’s name, design, texts, drawings and code belong to its author. Open-source libraries keep their own licences (licences).
  11. Manufacturers and errors. Scanok does not judge brands: it says what the label declares and which public criterion applies, with its source. You can report an error from a product’s page; if several people report the same product, what was contributed about it is deleted. Manufacturers and users can also write to the contact address; we reply and correct any error.

5. Health and belief data

Allergies, intolerances, conditions, diabetes and what you log in “Quit smoking” are health data, and some diets may reveal beliefs. Article 9 of the GDPR treats them as special categories. So:

  • You are asked for explicit consent (article 9.2.a), separate from the terms, before anything is stored.
  • “Quit smoking” has its own consent and its own delete button. The book is inside the app: reading it sends nothing. It is the author’s experience, not a treatment; the app links to official public-health resources.
  • This data never leaves the phone: not to the Scanok service, not to the AI, not to Open Food Facts.
  • You can withdraw consent at any time in Profile › What you avoid › Delete my exclusions: it is deleted at once.

No decision about allergens, diets or religion is made by artificial intelligence: they come from what the label declares and from published taxonomies, applied by the app’s engine.

7. The Scanok service

A small server of our own that helps read products. It runs in a Netcup GmbH data centre in Nuremberg (Germany), inside the European Union, isolated from any other service. It only answers requests signed by the app.

What it stores

  • Contributed products: barcode, country, name, brand, ingredient list and type (food or cosmetic), read from the photos someone sent. Only people scanning from that same country see them. It does not store who sent them. The photos are read and discarded immediately: they are never stored.
  • Lists already interpreted, alternatives already worked out for each product and country, and answers from public databases, so the work is not repeated. This is product data; what comes from public databases is refreshed every 30 days.
  • Error reports: barcode, country, reason and date. With three reports about the same product in one country, what was contributed in that country is deleted.
  • A technical log with no personal data: time, a fingerprint (hash) of the list or code, where the answer came from and whether anything failed.

Your IP address

  • No access log is kept.
  • To stop abuse, requests are counted per fingerprint of the address (not the address itself), in memory only and for one hour.
  • If an address goes over the limits, the server writes it to its error log and blocks it for one hour (security, legitimate interest, GDPR article 6.1.f). That log deletes itself after eight weeks at most.

Artificial intelligence

Reading the text or photos of a pack is done by Google Gemini (Google, on Scanok’s behalf). It only receives that text or those photos, never anything about you. Under the terms Google applies in Europe, it does not use them to improve its products; it keeps them for a limited time to detect abuse and may process them in data centres outside the European Union, with the safeguards of the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses. The AI only translates and classifies ingredients: it never sets the score, which is always calculated by the same Scanok engine.

That is why it matters: photograph only the pack, with no faces, rings, receipts, prescriptions or papers with personal details.

When you put a recipe in your weekly meal plan, Scanok schedules local notifications on your phone: the day before, to check the ingredients, and a while before cooking. They are created and shown inside the phone: there is no notification server and nothing is sent. They may appear on the lock screen; you can turn them off at any time in your phone’s settings.

  • The app never uses unencrypted connections.
  • The server only opens what is essential, keeps no access log and blocks abusers.
  • If a breach ever affected personal data, we would notify the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) and, where needed, those affected, as the GDPR requires.

16. Sources and content notices

Recipes and nutrition

Recipes are our own, from the free Wikibooks cookbook (CC BY-SA 3.0) or from the US NHLBI (public domain). Nutritional values come from USDA FoodData Central (public domain) and claims such as “high in protein” follow Regulation (EC) 1924/2006. They are calculations and legal thresholds, not personal advice.

Babies

For a baby, published criteria are used: the WHO Europe nutrient profile model (2022), the WHO recommendation of exclusive breastfeeding up to 6 months and the AESAN report on infant botulism (no honey before 12 months). Formula milk is not scored or compared. It does not replace your paediatrician.

Pets

Pet foods come from Open Pet Food Facts (ODbL). What is harmful to dogs and cats comes from the MSD Veterinary Manual, the FDA and EU feed-additive regulations. It does not replace your vet.

Medicines and supplements

Medicines are looked up in official lists that travel inside the app (AEMPS-CIMA in Spain, ANSM in France, URPL in Poland and the EMA for EU-wide authorisations): the search never leaves the phone. It says whether a medicine contains alcohol or anything of animal origin and anything you avoid. Only if you tap “See the official record” does your browser open the official website. It is information, not medical advice.

Recall alerts

AESAN recall alerts travel inside the app and are matched against your history on the phone.
